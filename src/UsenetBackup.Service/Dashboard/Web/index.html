Generate key secret

There's no passphrase in this mode. Instead, FileKeep generates a random 20-byte secret and programs it into your YubiKey's HMAC-SHA1 challenge–response slot. The answers the key computes from that secret are what encrypt your backups.

Generated during programming — never typed.
FileKeep creates the secret with a cryptographic random generator and writes it into each key when you program it. FileKeep itself keeps no copy. After programming, you'll print the secret on a recovery sheet — the only copy that exists outside your keys.
You never type this day to day — it's shown on your recovery sheet so you can recognize it.
No passphrase means no other way in.
If every programmed key is lost and the recovery sheet is gone, these backups cannot be decrypted — by anyone, including you. That's why the next steps let you program a spare and print the sheet.

Link your YubiKey

Insert your YubiKey, then touch its metal contact when it starts blinking. FileKeep sends it a challenge and stores the wrapped passphrase on this PC — the secret never leaves the key.

YubiKey and passphrase aren't linked
That means they work independently — either one unlocks on its own. Two common setups:
  • YubiKey holds your passphrase: Touch the key for daily unlocking; type your passphrase if the key is lost. The key is your fast path, the passphrase is your backup.
  • YubiKey as spare: Your passphrase is your primary unlock; the YubiKey is programmed as a backup you keep somewhere safe.
Slot 2 is the usual choice — slot 1 is often used by other sign-in features.
Slot 2 will be overwritten.
Programming the challenge–response secret replaces whatever is currently stored in that slot. If another app uses this slot on this key, it will stop working with that app.

Program your keys

Insert a YubiKey and touch its metal contact when it blinks — FileKeep writes the secret from the previous step into the key. Every key gets the same secret, so any programmed key can unlock your backups. One key is enough to get started. You can program a spare as a backup if you like — store it somewhere separate from your main key.
